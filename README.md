Available at: https://biohackers-psi.vercel.app/

Problem: In early-stage drug development, the preclinical drug discovery lag is a major bottleneck caused by challenges of identifying correct biological mechanism, modeling dynamic protein structures, and accurately screening identifying the correct biological mechanism is difficult; 40–50% of clinical failures are due to lack of efficacy, which points to poor target validation. 

This is where LEADS helps. Instead of scientists spending months manually combining genetic, literature and safety data, our agent ranks the proteins most strongly linked to a disease in minutes. It focuses on genetically supported targets, which are 2.6× more likely to reach approval. Adjustable weighting sliders let scientists decide which evidence matters most, and every score links back to its source, so the shortlist is fast, transparent and checkable. It furthermore suggests evaluating research to determine whether it is worth pursuing an experiment suggestion. The scientist approves the final target before it moves to target molecule screening which will keep human judgement at the centre. 

Who it helps: This tool intends to help Contract Research Organisation or sponsors in screening early drug research to cut down the time, streamlining the process, organisation and cutting down the time needed in preclinical research 

Data: will be taken from the open-source Open Target for the top 25 proteins linked to the disease. Data will be taken from chEMBL, which simulates and identifies and narrows down molecules that bind to the protein. Europe PMC and Clinical Trials. Gov.

Strengths: 
A multi-agent system that is also human-screened. Where once the protein scout is approved by the scientist, the screening agent opens that list: it ranks the actual molecules by potency and selectivity, checks predicted safety, and 2D structure of binding ligands
Accessible viewing and organisation, 
Compares past historical trial or research rejection to better guide researchers in designing experiments and trails
Generates a report for all findings and a citation list 
Limits: 
Open Target may be biased towards proteins that have been studied a lot. Newly discovered or potential proteins with less research on it may not appear as frequent. This may limit discovery
chEMBL is a platform more suited for small molecule drug discovery (to target proteins) may not be useful for gene therapy, but the initial stage of Open Target can still help scientists narrow the gene or protein causing down syndrome.


Safeguards: The screening agent automatically refuses any target that does not come with a human-approved record. This record contains the name of the researcher that approved it and the exact timestamp the target was approved. All evidence presented to the researcher comes with clickable links to real journal articles so that the information the agents gather can be verified by the researcher(s).
